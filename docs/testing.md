# How the checker and the agent are tested

The README gives the headline numbers. These are the full figures, run by run, including what still gets past the checker.

## The checker

**Checker** (`backend/src/wellpilot/agent/checker.py`): numbers are declared by the model (`{text, value, evidence_id}`) rather than parsed from prose; English and Norwegian number formats, unit scales and dates written in words are handled. It is tested five ways, all in CI except the two that call the model: property-based fuzzing over 10 number formats; a corpus of 488 real submissions replayed with their reviewed verdicts; metamorphic tests that reword every accepted answer in the corpus the way models vary their writing (typography, dates in words, "about", units, thousands separators: it must still pass) and break it in one checked way (a number, a date, a cause in a fact: it must fail); a paraphrase fuzzer (`python -m wellpilot.agent.paraphrase`) where Muse Spark Open writes true rewordings of the featured answers, 144 of them reviewed and pinned; and the red team, where it writes false ones. A false rejection used to surface only in a live run; the rewordings found nine before any run did. How often it catches a lie depends on who writes it: Muse Spark Open, attacking its own checker, wrote 90 false versions of the six answers featured on Oct 29 and 73 were caught (most of the rest are off by less than 1% after "about", or take a value from elsewhere in a dense table). A simulated reviewer (an AI agent prompted to break it) wrote 158 edits by hand: of the 110 false ones within what the checker judges, 79 are caught and 31 still pass, listed in `backend/tests/fixtures/panel7_probes.json`; of 32 true ones, 30 pass. A second simulated reviewer (Oct 29) tried new kinds of edit: of its 131 false ones in scope, 90 were caught and 41 passed on Oct 29 (units and quantities named in other words, numbers swapped between two jobs, a hedge that covers a cause), and of 54 true ones 48 pass. A third swapped each declared number of the featured answers for another value of the same result it cites, as a model that copied the wrong cell would: 85 of 151 passed when it ran, 39 after the fixes. The same swap over every accepted answer in the corpus (`backend/tests/checker_bench.py`, no model needed) went from 53% passing to 28% on Oct 29; on today's larger corpus 1,035 of 3,283 swaps pass (32%), and none of 580 true rewordings is rejected; the checker now compares what the words around a number say it is (before or after a change, P10 or P90, a month or a year, a limit, its unit, a plug's depth or a gauge's) with the value it was taken from. About a third of what still passes has no words that tell the two values apart. A fourth (Oct 30) aimed 128 false edits at kinds the earlier ones had not tried: 60 passed (47%; a number from another quantity in the same sentence, a percentage that contradicts its own from-to, before and after swapped), and 6 of 48 true edits were sent back. These are hand-written attacks on likely holes, not a base rate. So read the answer's check line ("every number, date and quote was found in the result it cites") as "every number was found where it says", not as "the reasoning is right".

## The agent

**Quality gates:** `python -m wellpilot.ingest.facts_gate` (free) checks the report facts: the plan's table rows, the design limits, the key quotes verbatim, no unverified quote, quotes located on their pages. `python -m wellpilot.eval` runs 15 real questions through the agent and checks each answer: verified, the right tools called, the facts any correct answer contains, what a correct answer must not say (a pressure collapse where the gauge died, a level between periods called a jump, an economic verdict with no costs in the data), claims in plain words, a step and cost budget. Latest full run (Nov 1, Muse Spark Open, $3.73): 12 of 15 verified, 9 pass every check; the other three verified answers went over a step or cost budget. Of the three unverified, one ended on a false rejection by the checker, fixed since (that answer now replays as accepted), one ran out of steps and one never declared its last numbers. Earlier runs: Oct 31, 9 of 15 verified, four of them false rejections fixed then; Oct 29, on a looser checker, 14 of 15. Ten further questions the eval had never held (Nov 1; written by a separate agent that read no code or docs, though the project's notes were in its context, so not strictly blind): 7 verified as run. Two of the other three were false rejections, fixed since, and now replay as verified; one was fair. Verified does not mean right: read against the data, 4 of the 7 held up, and 3 carried an error of meaning the checker cannot see (a summary calling a well's liquid steady where it jumped after a workover; the benefit of injection given to wells with no evidence of which injector reached which; all 2.48 million Sm3 after the plan's end credited to new wells, when F-12 and F-14 made 39% of it). The synthetic field (another field's data, though some checker fixes came from it): 5 of 5 verified, 4 pass every check; the fifth explained the decline by falling injection support without naming the injector that stopped. It is still a small, single-shot set made of the questions the checker was developed on.
